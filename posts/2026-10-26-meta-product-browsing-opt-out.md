Short answer: on 28 October 2026 Meta switches on product browsing for ads that are already running, unless you opt out. With it on, a tap on your ad can open a product experience inside Facebook or Instagram before anyone reaches your store. If you invested in your product pages and your landing pages, decide this on purpose this week. Don't let the default decide it for you.

## What is changing on 28 October?

Meta sent advertisers a notice in mid October. The headline reads: "Starting October 28, 2026, we'll add product browsing to your existing ads." The body says people who tap your ad "may see an experience on Facebook or Instagram with product info and personalized suggestions."

Product browsing itself is not new. It is one of the Advantage+ creative enhancements, listed as "Add product browsing". Meta's own help page on Advantage+ creative is clear about where it sends people: "This enhancement may send people to an experience on Facebook and Instagram first before your website."

What changes is the default. Until now you switched it on per ad. From 28 October it is on for existing ads unless you act.

A few things the notice does not say, and I won't guess at them:

- Whether new ads created after 28 October get it by default too. The notice only talks about "your existing ads".
- Which formats, placements or objectives are in scope.
- Whether the opt-out button covers one ad account or a whole business portfolio.

As of writing, I could not find a Meta Help Center article or newsroom post about the switch itself. The date and the wording come from the notice as advertisers received it.

## Why does it matter for a brand that built its own product page?

Because the tap is the moment your store starts working, and this setting can put a different page in that spot.

For a premium, design-led brand, the product page carries most of the sale. The imagery, the story, the size guide, the reviews, the offer and the reassurance all live there. A Meta page with "product info and personalized suggestions" is built from your catalog data, not from your page. It may also suggest other products than the one in the ad.

The setting also cuts into something the conversion side works hard on: congruency. The ad, the landing page and the offer should match on angle, imagery and price. On a Dutch skincare brand I work with, the Meta ads go to a dedicated landing page built for the ad angle, with a minimal header and the offer matched to the ad. An extra step on Meta's side sits between the ad and that page.

Then there is measurement. Your store analytics, your consent banner, your on-site tests and your session recordings all start when someone lands on your site. Anyone who browses on Meta's page and stops there never reaches them. So you get fewer sessions in Shopify and more activity that only Meta can see. Meta cites "3% more conversions" on average from its own test in August, but the notice doesn't say which conversions, counted where, or under which attribution window. A number from the platform that keeps the shopper is not the same as more orders in Shopify.

## Should you opt out?

There's no single right answer. That's the point of deciding before 28 October instead of finding out in November.

I'd lean towards opting out, or at least keeping it off until you've tested it, if:

- Your product pages or landing pages are where the sale really happens: rich imagery, a story, a buy box you have tested.
- Your ads point at a specific landing page or a pre-sell page, rather than a plain product page.
- Your catalog feed is thin or not kept up to date. Meta's page is only as good as that data.
- You judge Meta against Shopify orders and revenue per visitor, and you need that read to stay clean through Black Friday on 27 November.

Leaving it on makes more sense if:

- You sell many simple products where the catalog image and price do most of the work.
- Your ads already run as catalog or collection ads, so the shopper sees Meta's layer today anyway.
- You are willing to test it properly, with a holdout, and judge it on Shopify orders.

If you're unsure, opt out now and test it later on a small part of the spend. The notice says you can turn product browsing on or off "anytime", so opting out keeps the choice open. Leaving it on during the most expensive weeks of the year is harder to read and harder to undo cleanly.

## How do you opt out?

There are two routes, and I'd do both.

1. Use the button in the notice before 28 October. Look for the product browsing notice in your Meta notifications in Ads Manager or Business Suite. If you manage more than one ad account, check each one, because the notice does not say which account the button covers.
2. Check each running ad in Ads Manager. The notice says the switch lives in each ad's "Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings". Meta's help page on personalized destinations says that for ads in a campaign with Sales as the objective and Website as the conversion location, you'll find "Personalized destinations" under Destination, with an Edit link to turn features like product browsing on or off. Labels differ between accounts, so check both places on one ad first, then the rest.

After 28 October, check again. A duplicated ad is a new ad, and the notice does not promise how new ads behave. If your ads are created by a tool or an agency, ask them which enhancements they send with each new ad.

## How to check it worked

A 15-minute check after 28 October:

1. Open three of your top-spending ads in Ads Manager and confirm product browsing shows as off where you meant it to be off.
2. Tap each ad on your own phone in the Instagram and Facebook apps. You should land on your page, not a Meta product page.
3. Compare Meta sessions in Shopify analytics for the week after 28 October with the week before. A sudden drop with flat spend is the sign something got switched on.
4. Write down what you decided and why, so whoever runs the ads in November knows.

## Questions I get about this

### Does this affect my Shopify store setup?

No change is needed in Shopify. The setting lives in Meta. What you'll see in Shopify is the effect: fewer sessions from Meta if it's on and shoppers stay on Meta's page.

### Is product browsing the same as Shops ads or checkout on Meta?

No. Meta lists product browsing next to Shops as separate personalized destinations. Product browsing shows product details and suggestions on Facebook or Instagram, then sends people on to your website to buy.

### Will opting out hurt my results?

Meta says businesses saw 3% more conversions on average in its test. It doesn't define the metric, so I'd treat that as a reason to test, not a reason to leave it on. Test it on part of the spend and judge it on Shopify orders.

### I missed 28 October. Is it too late?

No. According to the notice you can switch it off per ad at any time. It just takes longer than one button.
