Short answer: should a Shopify brand hire someone in-house for conversion rate optimisation, or bring in a CRO agency or an independent specialist? Hire in-house when conversion work is weekly, core to how you sell, and you can keep one person busy and supported. Bring in outside help when you need a diagnosis, a test programme set up, or skills you only need part of the time. Most premium brands do best with a mix: one owner inside who decides, and outside people who research, build and test.

## First check: is it a conversion problem at all?

Before anyone hires anyone, look at where the headroom is. I once told a client team, in writing, that we did not have a broken website problem. Conversion and order value were roughly fine for where the brand was. The issue was traffic and new customers. A CRO hire in that situation would have spent money on the wrong side of the business.

I wrote more about this in [Most brands have a conversion problem, not a traffic problem](/blog/conversion-not-traffic-problem/). It goes both ways. Look at traffic, conversion rate, order value and returning share first. Then decide who you need.

Check the tracking too. If GA4 and Shopify don't agree on the order count, or a pixel stopped firing after a checkout change, a new CRO person will spend the first month fixing measurement. That is useful work, but know it going in. Conversion numbers can also move for reasons that have nothing to do with the store, as in [the autumn bot filtering change](/blog/conversion-rate-jump-bot-filtering/).

## What CRO work actually is

"CRO" is several jobs that often get bundled into one hire:

- **Research:** analytics, heatmaps, surveys, reviews and support tickets, where people drop off.
- **Priorities:** turning that research into a ranked list of changes and tests.
- **Design and copy:** the new product page section, the clearer offer, the better images.
- **Development:** building it in the theme, checkout extensions or an app, without slowing the store down.
- **Testing:** setting up the test, checking it works in every market, and calling the result honestly.

Few people are strong at all five. When a brand says "we need a CRO hire", the first question is which of these is missing.

## When an in-house hire makes sense

- You have the traffic to run tests most weeks, so there is real work every week.
- Conversion is part of how the brand sells, not a one-off project. Launches, campaigns and seasonal offers keep changing the store.
- Someone senior owns the results and protects the person's time from ad hoc requests.
- You can give them what they need: a testing app, analytics, and design and development support.

The risk with one in-house hire is that they end up alone. One person is expected to research, design, build and analyse, and ends up doing the part that is easiest to show. Plan the support around them, or the role turns into a banner and landing page job.

## When outside help makes sense

- You need a diagnosis first: where the store leaks and what to fix in what order.
- You want a test programme set up, and then run by your own team.
- The work is uneven: heavy before a launch or Black Friday, light in between.
- You need a specific skill for a while: checkout extensions, Shopify Functions, a theme rebuild, a speed audit.

## Agency or independent specialist?

An agency gives you a team and capacity. Research, design and development sit under one contract, and they can scale up for a big season. The trade-off is distance. Your store is one account of many, people rotate, and changes can land in your theme without anyone on your side knowing how they work.

An independent specialist gives you one senior person who knows your store well and is there every week. The trade-off is capacity. One person can't run a full redesign, a test programme and a checkout rebuild in the same month.

The way I work sits between the two. I work embedded, as part of the client team, with a simple ticket system, and content authoring stays with the client. That is a choice, and it doesn't fit every brand.

## The mix that works for most premium brands

One CRO operator I follow, who runs conversion work for brands in the 7 and 8 figure range, puts it simply: keep the core work in-house, usually the creative, outsource the rest, and check the output every week against clear goals. That matches what I see.

In practice:

- **Inside:** one owner who decides what gets tested and why, and who knows the brand. This can be the e-commerce lead. It doesn't have to be a new hire.
- **Outside:** the people who research, build and test, booked for the amount of work there actually is.
- **Every week:** a short check on what shipped, what you learned and what comes next.

## Agree on rules when several teams touch one store

The hidden cost of a mixed setup shows up in the theme. At one skincare brand, an external agency and my development work were changing the same theme, and there were no shared rules. Work got overwritten and nobody was sure what was live.

So I wrote a short collaboration contract for both sides:

- main always matches the live theme
- one branch and one pull request per task
- previews only on unpublished themes
- live pushes of changed files only

It fit on one page and removed most of the friction. If you bring in outside CRO help next to an existing developer or agency, agree on something like it on day one. More on release setups in [Single theme vs multi-theme releases](/blog/shopify-single-theme-vs-multi-theme-releases/).

## How to choose, in five questions

1. Is conversion really where the headroom is, and is the tracking right?
2. Which job is missing: research, priorities, design, development or testing?
3. Is there enough traffic and work to keep one person busy every week?
4. Who inside owns the result?
5. What happens to the theme when more than one team works on it?

If the answers point to part-time work and a missing skill, bring someone in. If they point to weekly work with a clear owner, hire. If you aren't sure yet, start with outside help for a few months and hire once you know what the role really is.

## Questions I get about this

### How much traffic do we need before a full-time CRO hire makes sense?

Enough to bring tests to a clear result most weeks. If one test needs two months to read, a full-time tester spends most of their time waiting. The [price testing post](/blog/ab-test-product-prices-shopify/) shows how traffic limits what you can test.

### Can our Shopify developer do CRO?

A good developer builds the changes and often spots friction others miss. Research and copy are usually different skills. Pair the developer with someone who owns the ideas.

### How long should an outside engagement run before we judge it?

About three months. That is enough to fix tracking, ship the first changes and read a first round of tests. Before that you are mostly judging the setup.

### Can a CRO agency and our developer work side by side?

Yes, if one person on your side owns the plan and both follow the same release rules. Without that, you pay twice for the same theme and argue about who broke what.
